Legally, It's Not a Spirit. Here's the Whole Story.

How a fermentation-first approach lets beer & wine venues pour full-strength cocktails — without touching their license.

Updated August 20264 min read

Walk into most neighborhood restaurants in California and you'll find the same quiet ceiling on the menu: beer, wine, maybe something low-proof that tastes like an apology. The reason isn't taste — it's the license. A Type 41 or Type 42 permit covers beer and wine, and a full-liquor Type 47 is expensive, scarce, and in many counties simply not available at any price.

But California doesn't define alcohol by how it tastes. It defines it by how it's made. Distillation produces a spirit. Fermentation does not. That single distinction — process, not flavor — is the entire foundation Novel is built on.

Every Novel cocktail begins as a clean, neutral fermented base. From there we build the drink the way a bar program would — real fruit, natural botanicals, balanced acid — up to a full 15.9% ABV. What comes out of the tap drinks exactly like a cocktail. What sits in the keg has never been anywhere near a still.

For an operator, that changes the math completely. The cocktail menu you thought required a six-figure license upgrade turns out to fit inside the license hanging on your wall right now. No new permits, no spirits on premises, no gray area — just a new tap handle and a category of revenue that used to belong exclusively to full bars.

And because it pours from a draft line, you get something even a full bar struggles with: a perfect, identical cocktail in about ten seconds, from any staff member on shift, 123 times per sixtel.

We share full product and compliance documentation with every account during onboarding, so your team is never guessing. If you run a beer & wine venue on the Central Coast and want to see the pour in person, that's exactly what our tastings are for.

The short version

California classifies alcohol by production method — fermentation versus distillation — not by strength or flavor. A fermented drink built to full cocktail strength stays inside the beer & wine category a Type 41 or Type 42 license already authorizes.
